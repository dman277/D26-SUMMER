The move: Flaw + Assumption + Inference = 40%+ of all LR questions. Master those three and you're already ahead of most test-takers. Then add Strengthen + Weaken and you own 60%+.
7-Month Study Phases
May – June · Month 1–2
Foundation
Learn test structure. Master Flaw + Assumption. Know all 14 question types. Start Logic Games basics. Khan Academy + 7Sage free tier.
July – August · Month 3–4
Volume + Drills
Timed drills every section. 1 full practice test/week. Find weak spots. Get a tutor locked in. Use LawHub official tests.
September – November · Month 5–7
Test Mode
2–3 full PTs per week. Full wrong-answer review. Simulate real conditions. Refine until score is locked. December = show time.

Writing
LSAT Argumentative Writing
Separate. Not scored but sent to all law schools. Don't sleep on it.
Question Types · Ranked by Frequency
#1
Assumption Most Common
What must be true for the argument to hold? Negate it to check. Master this first.
#2
Flaw in Reasoning
What logical error does the argument make? Correlation vs causation, hasty generalization, false dichotomy, circular reasoning.
#3
Inference / Must Be True
What MUST follow from the passage? Be conservative — pick the safest, most supported answer.
#4
Strengthen
Add info that makes the conclusion more likely. Appears ~2x more than Weaken on recent tests.
#5
Weaken
Attack the gap between premise and conclusion. Flip side of Strengthen — study both together.
#6
Paradox / Resolve
Two seemingly contradictory facts. Find what explains how both can be true simultaneously.
#7+
Parallel · Principle · Method · Point at Issue · Role
Less frequent but still tested. Master the top 5 first, then tackle these.
Terminology Dictionary
Conclusion
The main claim being proven. Look for: "therefore," "thus," "so," "hence," "clearly."
Premise
Evidence supporting the conclusion. Look for: "because," "since," "given that."
Necessary Assumption
Must be true for the argument to hold. If false, the whole argument falls. Use the Negation Test.
Sufficient Assumption
If true, GUARANTEES the conclusion. Bridges the gap between evidence and conclusion completely.
Conditional Logic
If A→B. Does NOT mean B→A. Contrapositive: NOT B → NOT A. Always valid.
Contrapositive
Logically equivalent flip. If A→B, then NOT B→NOT A. Write it automatically.
Correlation vs. Causation
Just because A and B happen together doesn't mean A caused B. #1 most common LSAT flaw.
Hasty Generalization
Broad conclusion from too small a sample. "3 students failed so the whole class struggled."
False Dichotomy
Only two options presented when more exist. "Either with us or against us."
Circular Reasoning
Using the conclusion as a premise. The argument assumes what it's trying to prove.
Ad Hominem
Attacking the person instead of the argument. Irrelevant to the logic itself.
Negation Test
Negate an answer choice. If it destroys the argument → it's the Necessary Assumption.

04
